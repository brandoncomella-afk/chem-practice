Water, Polarity, and Dissolving

Question 1Beginner

Water is called the universal solvent because it is a polar molecule. Which statement explains why a water molecule is polar?

  1. A. The oxygen atom pulls the shared electrons more strongly, so it takes a partial negative charge
  2. B. The oxygen atom has a lower electronegativity than hydrogen, so it takes a partial positive charge
  3. C. The hydrogen atoms give their electrons away completely, so the molecule is built out of two ions
  4. D. The oxygen atom and the hydrogen atoms share the electrons perfectly evenly among the three of them
Show answer
Answer: A. The oxygen atom pulls the shared electrons more strongly, so it takes a partial negative charge
Oxygen has the higher electronegativity, so it pulls the shared electrons closer and takes a partial negative charge while the hydrogen ends take a partial positive charge. Perfectly even sharing would make the molecule nonpolar, giving electrons away completely would build ions rather than a covalent molecule, and oxygen's electronegativity is higher than hydrogen's, not lower.
Question 2Beginner

A student shakes cooking oil with water in a beaker, and the two layers separate again as soon as the shaking stops. Why does the oil not dissolve?

  1. A. Oil is lighter than water, and a substance that floats can never dissolve at all
  2. B. Oil is nonpolar, so the polar water molecules are not attracted to it
  3. C. Oil is polar, so the polar water molecules push it away into its own layer
  4. D. Oil is an ionic compound, and ionic compounds dissolve only in nonpolar liquids
Show answer
Answer: B. Oil is nonpolar, so the polar water molecules are not attracted to it
Oil is nonpolar, so the partial charges on water find nothing to attract and the oil stays in its own layer. Calling oil polar gets the property backwards, oil is a molecular substance rather than an ionic compound, and floating has nothing to do with whether a solute dissolves.
Question 3Beginner

Table salt dissolves because polar water molecules pull the ions apart. Which end of a water molecule is attracted to a chloride ion, Cl⁻?

  1. A. The hydrogen end, which carries a partial positive charge
  2. B. The oxygen end, which carries a partial positive charge
  3. C. The oxygen end, which carries a full negative charge of −1
  4. D. The hydrogen end, which carries a partial negative charge
Show answer
Answer: A. The hydrogen end, which carries a partial positive charge
A chloride ion is negative, so it is attracted to the hydrogen end of water, which carries the partial positive charge. The oxygen end carries the partial negative charge and attracts positive ions instead, and the charges on water are partial charges from unequal sharing, never a full −1.
Question 4Intermediate

A fuel-oil sheen floats on the river while the dissolved salts in that same water stay in solution. Which explanation accounts for both observations?

  1. A. Oil is the more polar of the two, so the partial charges on the water molecules push it away to the surface
  2. B. Salt is nonpolar, so it slips between the water molecules, while the polar oil is blocked at the surface
  3. C. Water's partial charges hold the oil up in a layer, and the salts stay under only because they are heavier
  4. D. Water's partial charges pull the ions of the salt apart, but nonpolar oil gives them nothing to pull on
Show answer
Answer: D. Water's partial charges pull the ions of the salt apart, but nonpolar oil gives them nothing to pull on
Unequal sharing of the electrons gives water a partial positive end and a partial negative end, and those partial charges pull the ions of a salt apart and surround them, while an oil molecule shares its electrons nearly evenly and offers nothing to grip. Oil is the nonpolar substance and salt the ionic one, not the reverse, and the separation has nothing to do with which is heavier.

Solute, Solvent, and Solution

Question 5Beginner

In the drink-mix lab you stir a spoonful of powdered drink mix into a beaker of water. Which substance is the solute?

  1. A. The drink mix, because it is the substance being dissolved
  2. B. The stirring rod, because it is what breaks the powder apart
  3. C. The water, because it is the substance being dissolved by the powder
  4. D. The finished drink, because a solution counts as its own solute
Show answer
Answer: A. The drink mix, because it is the substance being dissolved
The solute is the substance being dissolved, which here is the powdered drink mix; the water is the solvent doing the dissolving. The finished drink is the solution the two of them make, and the stirring rod is equipment that only speeds the dissolving up.

The solvent is the substance present in the larger amount, and it is what does the dissolving. The substance in the smaller amount is the solute, the evenly mixed combination is the solution itself, and undissolved solid on the bottom is simply solute that never went in.

Electrolytes and Nonelectrolytes

Question 9Beginner

A conductivity tester is dipped into a beaker of salt water and the bulb lights up. What does that tell you about the solution?

  1. A. The tester has identified exactly which ion is in the water
  2. B. Dissolved ions are present in the solution to carry the current
  3. C. Dissolved sugar is present, the one solute that carries a current
  4. D. Dissolved molecules are present, and molecules carry current best
Show answer
Answer: B. Dissolved ions are present in the solution to carry the current
A lit bulb means an ionic compound dissolved and dissociated, so dissolved ions are present to carry the current. The tester cannot say which ion is there, dissolved sugar leaves the bulb dark because it does not ionize, and whole molecules do not carry a current.
Question 10Beginner

Sugar dissolves completely in a beaker of water, but the conductivity tester stays dark. What does that make sugar?

  1. A. A nonelectrolyte, because it dissolves without breaking into ions
  2. B. An electrolyte, because anything that dissolves in water must form ions
  3. C. An electrolyte, because its molecules carry a current once they spread out
  4. D. A nonelectrolyte, because it never truly dissolves in water in the first place
Show answer
Answer: A. A nonelectrolyte, because it dissolves without breaking into ions
Sugar dissolves but does not ionize, so no ions are there to carry a current and sugar is a nonelectrolyte. Dissolving does not by itself produce ions, whole sugar molecules carry no current, and the sugar plainly did dissolve, since the liquid is clear.
Question 11Beginner

NaCl is an ionic compound. What does it do when it dissolves in water?

  1. A. It stays whole, drifting through the water as intact NaCl units
  2. B. It reacts with the water and is destroyed, leaving only pure water
  3. C. It dissociates in water, breaking apart into its component ions
  4. D. It ionizes into neutral sodium and chlorine atoms that float apart
Show answer
Answer: C. It dissociates in water, breaking apart into its component ions
An ionic compound dissociates in water, which means it breaks apart into its component ions, and those ions are what a conductivity tester detects. Intact NaCl units would leave the bulb dark, the particles released are charged ions rather than neutral atoms, and the salt is not destroyed, since evaporating the water brings it back.

Saturated, Unsaturated, and Supersaturated

Question 14Beginner

A beaker of water at 25 °C has extra table salt sitting undissolved on the bottom after standing overnight. How is this solution described?

  1. A. Dilute, because the leftover solid shows very little salt actually dissolved
  2. B. Saturated, because it holds all the solute it can at that temperature
  3. C. Unsaturated, because there is still solid sitting on the bottom of it
  4. D. Supersaturated, because it holds more solute than it normally could
Show answer
Answer: B. Saturated, because it holds all the solute it can at that temperature
A solution sitting at its saturation point with undissolved solute left over is saturated: it holds all the solute it can at that temperature. Unsaturated would mean more could still dissolve, supersaturated means holding more than the saturation point after cooling undisturbed, and the leftover solid shows the water is full rather than dilute.

  2. B. Saturated, because all 50. g of Compound W went into solution
  3. C. Unsaturated, because more of Compound W could still dissolve
  4. D. Supersaturated, because Compound W dissolved with no heating
Show answer
Answer: C. Unsaturated, because more of Compound W could still dissolve
The curve allows 145 g per 100. mL at 20 °C and only 50. g is in there, so the solution is unsaturated and has room for more. It is not saturated, since the saturation point at 20 °C is 145 g rather than 50. g, and supersaturated would mean holding more than 145 g after cooling undisturbed.
Question 16Beginner

Which procedure produces a supersaturated solution?

  1. A. Dissolve solute in hot water, then let it cool slowly and undisturbed
  2. B. Stir a saturated solution much harder until extra solute is forced in
  3. C. Add extra solute to hot water and keep heating it until it all goes in
  4. D. Grind the solute into a fine powder so that more of it can dissolve
Show answer
Answer: A. Dissolve solute in hot water, then let it cool slowly and undisturbed
Supersaturation appears only when a hot solution cools slowly with nothing to start crystals, so it briefly holds more solute than its saturation point allows. Adding solute while heating just gives a hot saturated or unsaturated solution, and stirring harder or grinding the solid finer changes only the dissolving rate, never how much can dissolve.

A supersaturated solution is unstable and needs only a seed surface for crystallization to begin, and the thermometer provides one.
