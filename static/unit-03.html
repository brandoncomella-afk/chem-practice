Periodic Table Organization

Question 1Beginner

On the periodic table, the vertical columns are called:

  1. A. blocks, named for the s, p, d, and f orbitals
  2. B. groups, also known as the chemical families
  3. C. periods, the same word used for the table's rows
  4. D. energy levels, one level for each row shown
Show answer
Answer: B. groups, also known as the chemical families
On the periodic table, columns are called groups, and the objectives also call them chemical families. Rows are the periods, so calling a column a period swaps the two words; the s, p, d, and f blocks run across several columns, and an energy level is a place for electrons, not a part of the chart.
Question 2Beginner

Silicon (Si) sits in the same column as tin (Sn), two rows above it. Silicon and tin are therefore in the same:

  1. A. f block
  2. B. d block
  3. C. group
  4. D. period
Show answer
Answer: C. group
Elements stacked in the same column belong to the same group, which is why silicon and tin both have 4 valence electrons. Silicon is in Period 3 while tin is in Period 5, so they are not in the same period, and both are p block elements rather than d block or f block elements.
Question 3Beginner

Groups 1 and 2, on the far left of the periodic table, make up which block?

  1. A. the s block
  2. B. the f block
  3. C. the p block
  4. D. the d block
Show answer
Answer: A. the s block
The valence electrons of Groups 1 and 2 go into an s sublevel, which is why sodium ends in 3s¹ and calcium ends in 4s². The p block holds Groups 13 through 18, the d block holds the transition metals in the wide middle, and the f block is the pair of rows pulled out at the bottom.
Question 4Beginner

Which statement correctly describes the layout of the periodic table?

  2. B. The columns are called periods and the rows are called groups.
  3. C. The columns are called groups and the rows are called periods.
  4. D. The columns are called orbitals and the rows are called shells.

Answer: C. The columns are called groups and the rows are called periods.
Columns are groups and rows are periods, so tin sits in Group 14 and Period 5. Saying the columns are periods and the rows are groups reverses both words, and blocks, families, orbitals, and shells are not the names used for the rows of the chart.
Question 5Beginner

Potassium (K) is found in Period 4 of the periodic table. How many energy levels hold electrons in a neutral potassium atom?

  1. A. 19
  2. B. 8
  3. C. 2
  4. D. 4
Show answer
Answer: D. 4
The period number tells you how many energy levels hold electrons, so a Period 4 element such as potassium uses 4 energy levels. The value 19 is potassium's atomic number, which is its proton count and its electron count, and 8 is the number of electrons the second energy level can hold.

Question 9Intermediate

Tin's noble gas configuration is [Kr]4d¹⁰5s²5p². Which block of the periodic table is tin in, and which period?

  1. A. The p block, Period 5, because the last electrons enter 5p
  2. B. The p block, Period 4, because the highest d sublevel is 4d
  3. C. The d block, Period 4, because the 4d sublevel is filled
  4. D. The d block, Period 5, because tin holds ten d electrons
Show answer
Answer: A. The p block, Period 5, because the last electrons enter 5p
The block is named for the sublevel the last electrons enter, which is 5p, and the period number is the highest energy level used, which is 5. Calling tin a d-block element counts the filled 4d¹⁰ core instead of the outermost sublevel, and reading Period 4 off the '4d' ignores the 5s and 5p electrons entirely.
Question 10Intermediate

Period 3 runs from sodium (11) through argon (18), and Period 4 runs from potassium (19) through krypton (36). How many elements are in each period, and what accounts for the difference?

  1. A. 8 in Period 3 and 10 in Period 4; the 3d sublevel replaces the 4p
  2. B. 8 in Period 3 and 18 in Period 4; the 4f sublevel adds 10 more
  3. C. 8 in Period 3 and 18 in Period 4; the 3d sublevel adds 10 more
  4. D. 18 in Period 3 and 18 in Period 4; both fill an s, p, and d sublevel
Show answer
Answer: C. 8 in Period 3 and 18 in Period 4; the 3d sublevel adds 10 more
Sodium through argon is 18 − 11 + 1 = 8 elements (3s² then 3p⁶), while potassium through krypton is 36 − 19 + 1 = 18 elements because 4s², 3d¹⁰, and 4p⁶ all fill. The 4f sublevel does not begin until Period 6, and Period 3 has no d sublevel at all, so it cannot hold 18 elements.
Question 11Advanced

Period 4 of the periodic table holds 18 elements while Period 3 holds only 8. Using the sublevel capacities (s = 2, p = 6, d = 10, f = 14) and the order in which the sublevels actually fill, which explanation accounts for the jump from 8 to 18?

  1. A. Period 4 fills 3d, then 3p, then 4s, and 10 + 6 + 2 gives the 18 elements in that row
  2. B. Period 4 fills 4s and 4p only, and 2 + 6 gives 8, with the ten d elements counted in Period 3
  3. C. Period 4 fills 4s, then 4d, then 4p, and 2 + 10 + 6 gives the 18 elements in that row
  4. D. Period 4 fills 4s, then 3d, then 4p, and 2 + 10 + 6 gives the 18 elements in that row
Show answer
Answer: D. Period 4 fills 4s, then 3d, then 4p, and 2 + 10 + 6 gives the 18 elements in that row
Electrons take the lowest available sublevel first, and 4s is lower than 3d, so the row runs 4s, then 3d, then 4p: 2 + 10 + 6 = 18. Writing 4d in place of 3d skips a whole sublevel and would leave 3d permanently empty, and filling 3p during Period 4 is impossible because 3p was already full at the end of Period 3.

Chemical Families

Question 13Beginner

Sodium (Na) and potassium (K) both sit in Group 1. What is this group of elements called?

  1. A. the alkali metals
  2. B. the alkaline earth metals
  3. C. the transition metals
  4. D. the noble gases
Show answer
Answer: A. the alkali metals
Group 1 is the alkali metals, each with 1 valence electron in an s orbital and each very reactive in water. The alkaline earth metals are Group 2, the transition metals fill the middle of the table, and the noble gases are Group 18.
Question 14Beginner

The halogens are found in Group 17 of the periodic table. How many valence electrons does a halogen atom have?

  1. A. 1
  2. B. 8
  3. C. 7
  4. D. 2
Show answer
Answer: C. 7
A halogen has 7 valence electrons, which you can count in chlorine's configuration 1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁵ as 2 + 5 in the third energy level. A count of 8 belongs to the noble gases of Group 18, while 1 and 2 belong to the alkali metals and the alkaline earth metals.
Question 15Beginner

  2. B. They are gases that are not reactive.
  3. C. They are gases that react quickly with water.
  4. D. They are shiny solids that are malleable.

Answer: B. They are gases that are not reactive.
The Group 18 noble gases are gases at room temperature and are not reactive, which is why argon was named for the Greek word for lazy. Shiny, malleable solids describe the metal families, and brittle solids that react quickly describe the halogens rather than the noble gases.
Question 16Beginner

Where are the transition metals found on the periodic table?

  1. A. in Groups 1 and 2 only
  2. B. in Groups 13 through 18
  3. C. in Groups 3 through 12
  4. D. in Group 17 and Group 18
Show answer
Answer: C. in Groups 3 through 12
The transition metals fill the wide middle section of the table, Groups 3 through 12, and each has 1 or 2 valence electrons in an s orbital. Groups 1 and 2 are the alkali and alkaline earth metals, and Groups 13 through 18 include the halogens and the noble gases.

The alkaline earth metals are reactive in water and very reactive in acid, which is exactly the pattern seen when the acid was added. Question 22Intermediate

The Day 8 handout says the noble gases cannot have their atomic radius measured the same way as other elements. Which statement gives the reason together with the electron structure behind it?

  1. A. They have an incomplete d sublevel, so the outer edge of the atom keeps changing
  2. B. They hold just one valence electron, so any bond they form is too weak to measure
  3. C. They have a full outer energy level, so they generally do not bond with other atoms
  4. D. They are all radioactive gases, so a sample never sits still long enough to measure
Show answer
Answer: C. They have a full outer energy level, so they generally do not bond with other atoms
Atomic radius is defined as half the distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms, so an element that does not bond gives you no way to locate its edge, and the noble gases have full s and p sublevels (8 valence electrons, 2 for helium). Radon is the only radioactive member of the family, and noble gases have neither a lone valence electron nor a partly filled d sublevel.
Question 23Intermediate

In the Dr. Rrrg puzzle, element 'Hu' is described as having the greatest electronegativity of all of the elements, and element 'Hf' as having the highest first ionization energy of all of the elements. Which families do Hu and Hf belong to?

  1. A. Hu is a noble gas and Hf is a halogen
  2. B. Hu is a halogen and Hf is a noble gas
  3. C. Hu is a halogen and Hf is an alkali metal
  4. D. Hu is an alkaline earth metal and Hf is a halogen
Show answer
Answer: B. Hu is a halogen and Hf is a noble gas
Electronegativity increases to the right and up a group, so its maximum is fluorine at the top of the halogens, while first ionization energy peaks at helium, a noble gas. Question 24Advanced

Two clues in the Dr. Rrrg puzzle read: “Hu has the greatest electronegativity of all of the elements” and “Hf has the highest first ionization energy of all of the elements.” Which real elements are Hu and Hf, and what families are they in?

  1. A. Hu is fluorine, a halogen, and Hf is francium, an alkali metal
  2. B. Hu is oxygen, a nonmetal, and Hf is helium, one of the noble gases
  3. C. Hu is chlorine, a halogen, and Hf is neon, one of the noble gases
  4. D. Hu is fluorine, a halogen, and Hf is helium, a noble gas
Show answer
Answer: D. Hu is fluorine, a halogen, and Hf is helium, a noble gas
Electronegativity increases across a period from left to right and up a group, which puts fluorine at the extreme and makes it the most electronegative element, ahead of both chlorine and oxygen. First ionization energy follows the same directions, and helium tops Group 18 with a full first energy level, so its first ionization energy is the highest of any element; francium sits at the opposite corner and has one of the lowest.
Question 25Advanced

In the alkaline earth lab, magnesium and calcium are each placed in 2 cm of distilled water with 3 drops of phenolphthalein. Calcium fizzes and the water turns bright pink; magnesium barely changes. A group is then handed beryllium, which sits directly above magnesium. What should they predict?

  1. A. Beryllium reacts faster than magnesium, because a smaller atom always reacts faster in water
  2. B. Beryllium reacts faster than calcium, because it has the fewest energy levels of the three metals
  3. C. Beryllium reacts the same as magnesium, because both are alkaline earth metals in the same family
  4. D. Beryllium reacts even less than magnesium, because its valence electrons are held closest to the nucleus
Show answer
Answer: D. Beryllium reacts even less than magnesium, because its valence electrons are held closest to the nucleus
Reactivity of a metal tracks its first ionization energy, which RISES going up a group: beryllium has the fewest energy levels, so its valence electrons sit closest to the nucleus and are hardest to remove. Since magnesium already barely reacted, beryllium should react even less. Being in the same family does not make two metals equally reactive, as magnesium and calcium themselves demonstrated.

Metals, Nonmetals, and Metalloids

Question 27Beginner

Which set of properties describes the metals on the periodic table?

  1. A. Earthy luster, malleable and ductile, and good conductors
  2. B. Metallic luster, brittle, and poor conductors of electricity
  3. C. Earthy luster, brittle, and usually gases at room temperature
  4. D. Metallic luster, malleable and ductile, and good conductors
Show answer
Answer: D. Metallic luster, malleable and ductile, and good conductors
Metals have a metallic luster, can be hammered into sheets and drawn into wire, and conduct both heat and electricity well. Being brittle and a poor conductor describes a nonmetal, and an earthy luster is what students saw on the sulfur and carbon samples, not on the copper or magnesium.
Question 28Beginner

A sample of sulfur has a dull, earthy luster, crumbles when it is hammered, and does not light the conductivity tester. Sulfur is best classified as:

  1. A. a metal, since only metals are solid
  2. B. a nonmetal, since it is dull and brittle
  3. C. a noble gas, since it is not reactive
  4. D. a metalloid, since it is a solid element
Show answer
Answer: B. a nonmetal, since it is dull and brittle
No metallic luster, no malleability, and no conductivity are all nonmetal properties, so sulfur is a nonmetal. A metalloid would still show a metallic luster and would conduct at least weakly, many nonmetals are solids too, and the noble gases are all gases in Group 18.
Question 29Beginner

Metalloids sit along the stair-step line of the periodic table. Which statement describes them?

  1. A. They have no luster and never conduct electricity.
  2. B. They are gases at room temperature like nonmetals.
  3. C. They have properties of metals and of nonmetals.
  4. D. They have every property that a true metal has.
Show answer
Answer: C. They have properties of metals and of nonmetals.
Metalloids such as silicon have a metallic luster and conduct heat and electricity, but not nearly as well as a metal, and they are brittle rather than malleable. That mix is why they are neither full metals nor full nonmetals, and metalloids are solids at room temperature, not gases.
Question 30Beginner

Metalloids such as silicon can be used as semi-conductors. What does a semi-conductor do?

  2. B. It stops conducting electricity as soon as it is heated.
  3. C. It conducts electricity better as its temperature increases.
  4. D. It conducts heat well but never conducts any electric current.

Answer: C. It conducts electricity better as its temperature increases.
A semi-conductor conducts electricity better as its temperature increases, which is what makes silicon so useful inside a phone. Losing conductivity when heated is the opposite of that behavior, hammering changes shape rather than conductivity, and a semi-conductor does carry an electric current.
