Counting Atoms in a Formula

Question 1Beginner

One of the pills left in the vial is acetaminophen, C₈H₉NO₂. How many hydrogen atoms are in one molecule of acetaminophen?

  1. A. 2 hydrogen atoms
  2. B. 8 hydrogen atoms
  3. C. 9 hydrogen atoms
  4. D. 1 hydrogen atom
Show answer
Answer: C. 9 hydrogen atoms
The subscript written after an element tells you how many atoms of that element there are, and the subscript after H is 9. The 8 belongs to carbon and the 2 belongs to oxygen, and 1 would only be correct if hydrogen had no subscript at all.

The Mole Concept

Question 9Beginner

A pair of shoes is always 2 shoes and a dozen roses is always 12 roses. A mole of atoms is always ______.

  1. A. 6.022 × 10⁻²³ atoms
  2. B. 6.022 atoms
  3. C. 6.022 × 10²³ atoms
  4. D. 6.022 × 10²² atoms
Show answer
Answer: C. 6.022 × 10²³ atoms
A mole is a counting word, and the count it stands for is 6.022 × 10²³ particles. Answering 6.022 atoms keeps the digits but throws away the power of ten, and 6.022 × 10²² is ten times too small.
Question 10Beginner

A mole connects ______.

  1. A. the number of protons to the number of neutrons
  2. B. the mass of a substance to the particles in it
  3. C. the oxidation number to the valence number
  4. D. the number of particles to the atomic number
Show answer
Answer: B. the mass of a substance to the particles in it
The mole is the bridge between a mass you can weigh on a balance and the number of particles in that sample, which is why 1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles = the mass in grams from the periodic table. Protons and neutrons, oxidation numbers, and atomic numbers are all counts of subatomic particles or charges, and none of them are tied to the mole.
Question 11Beginner

Which statement about the mole is correct?

  1. A. One mole always means 12 particles, just like a dozen
  2. B. One mole of any substance contains the same number of particles
  3. C. One mole of a heavy element holds more particles than a light one
  4. D. One mole of any substance has the same mass in grams
Show answer
Answer: B. One mole of any substance contains the same number of particles
A mole is a fixed count, 6.022 × 10²³ particles, no matter what the substance is. The masses are not the same, because a heavier atom makes a heavier mole, and a dozen is 12 while a mole is 6.022 × 10²³.

Question 13Beginner

Potassium cyanide, KCN, is an ionic compound. One mole of KCN contains 6.022 × 10²³ particles, and those particles are correctly called:

  1. A. molecules, because KCN is a compound
  2. B. formula units, because KCN is ionic
  3. C. atoms, because KCN holds three elements
  4. D. grams, because KCN can be weighed out

Answer: B. formula units, because KCN is ionic
Ionic compounds such as KCN and NaCl are counted in formula units, because they are not built from separate molecules the way C₈H₉NO₂ is. One formula unit of KCN contains 3 atoms, so an atom count is not the same as a particle count, and grams measure mass rather than a number of particles.

Question 15Beginner

Molar Mass

Question 31Beginner

The mass of one mole of a substance, reported in g/mol, is called its ______.

  1. A. percent composition
  2. B. empirical formula
  3. C. formula mass
  4. D. molar mass
Show answer
Answer: D. molar mass
Molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance and carries the units g/mol. Formula mass is the same count of atoms reported in amu for a single molecule, while percent composition is a percent and an empirical formula is a ratio of atoms, so neither is a mass.

  4. D. 0.0607 mL, which fits easily inside the capsule
Show answer
Answer: A. 0.0595 mL, which fits easily inside the capsule
Rearranging density = mass / volume gives volume = 0.0601 g × (1 mL / 1.01 g) = 0.059505 mL, or 0.0595 mL to the three significant digits both measurements supply — well under the 0.5 mL the capsule holds, so nicotine cannot be ruled out on volume.
