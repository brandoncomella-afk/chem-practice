Reading a Balanced Equation

Question 1Beginner

The Alchemist chalks the recipe for the Aqua Verde reaction on the wall of the workshop: 3CuSO₄(aq) + 2Al(s) → Al₂(SO₄)₃(aq) + 3Cu(s). How many moles of aluminum does this recipe call for?

  1. A. 6 mol Al
  2. B. 3 mol Al
  3. C. 1 mol Al
  4. D. 2 mol Al
Show answer
Answer: D. 2 mol Al
The number written in front of a formula is its coefficient, and it tells how many moles of that substance the recipe uses; the 2 sits in front of Al. Reading 1 mol Al treats aluminum as though it had no coefficient at all, 3 mol is what stands in front of CuSO₄ and Cu, and 6 mol comes from multiplying two coefficients together.
Question 2Beginner

In the Sack of Silver reaction you write 2AgNO₃(aq) + Cu(s) → Cu(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2Ag(s). Which substance is a product that appears as a solid metal?

  1. A. Cu(s), the copper wire used up
  2. B. AgNO₃(aq), the dissolved reactant
  3. C. Ag(s), the silver that forms
  4. D. Cu(NO₃)₂(aq), the dissolved product
Show answer
Answer: C. Ag(s), the silver that forms
Products are written to the right of the arrow, and Ag(s) is the only substance on the right carrying an (s) for solid. Cu(s) is a solid but sits on the left as a reactant, Cu(NO₃)₂(aq) is a product yet stays dissolved, and AgNO₃(aq) is a dissolved reactant.
Question 3Beginner

A strip of magnesium flashes in the Alchemist's furnace: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO. If 4.86 g of magnesium reacts completely with 3.20 g of oxygen gas, what mass of magnesium oxide forms?

  1. A. 8.1 g
  2. B. 1.66 g
  3. C. 16.1 g
  4. D. 8.06 g
Show answer
Answer: D. 8.06 g
Mass is conserved, so every gram that goes in comes back out as product: 4.86 g + 3.20 g = 8.06 g of MgO. Adding is limited by decimal places rather than significant digits, and both masses run to the hundredths place, so 8.1 g throws away a digit that was measured; 1.66 g comes from subtracting and 16.1 g from doubling the total.
Question 4Beginner

The rusting recipe from Lemac's iron is 4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃. How many oxygen atoms are shown on the product side of this equation?

  1. A. 12 oxygen atoms
  2. B. 5 oxygen atoms
  3. C. 6 oxygen atoms
  4. D. 3 oxygen atoms
Show answer
Answer: C. 6 oxygen atoms
On the product side the coefficient 2 multiplies the 3 oxygens inside Fe₂O₃, giving 2 × 3 = 6 oxygen atoms, which matches the 3 × 2 = 6 on the reactant side. Answering 3 reads only the subscript and ignores the coefficient, 5 adds 2 + 3 instead of multiplying, and 12 multiplies by the 4 in front of Fe.

  3. C. Fe + CuCl₂ → FeCl₃ + Cu
  4. D. 4Fe + 6CuCl₂ → 4FeCl₃ + 6Cu

Answer: B. 2Fe + 3CuCl₂ → 2FeCl₃ + 3Cu
Counting atoms in 2Fe + 3CuCl₂ → 2FeCl₃ + 3Cu gives 2 Fe, 3 Cu and 6 Cl on each side. Question 8Beginner

Lemac hands over gold (III) chloride and a ribbon of magnesium: 2AuCl₃(aq) + 3Mg(s) → 3MgCl₂(aq) + 2Au(s). What type of reaction is this?

  1. A. Synthesis, two elements combine to build one compound
  2. B. Single replacement, one metal takes the other's place
  3. C. Double replacement, two compounds trade their partners
  4. D. Decomposition, one compound breaks into simpler pieces
Show answer
Answer: B. Single replacement, one metal takes the other's place
An element and a compound react and the magnesium takes the chlorine away from the gold, leaving gold as a free element, which is the pattern of a single replacement. A double replacement needs two compounds on the left, a decomposition needs a single compound on the left, and a synthesis would have one substance on the right.
Question 9Intermediate

The Alchemist writes the rusting recipe as 4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃. For the Philosopher's Stone he needs exactly 8 mol of Fe₂O₃, so the whole recipe has to be scaled up. Which scaled recipe is correct?

  1. A. 16 mol Fe + 12 mol O₂ → 8 mol Fe₂O₃
  2. B. 4 mol Fe + 3 mol O₂ → 8 mol Fe₂O₃
  3. C. 12 mol Fe + 16 mol O₂ → 8 mol Fe₂O₃
  4. D. 8 mol Fe + 6 mol O₂ → 8 mol Fe₂O₃
Show answer
Answer: A. 16 mol Fe + 12 mol O₂ → 8 mol Fe₂O₃
Eight moles of Fe₂O₃ is 4 times the 2 mol in the recipe, so every coefficient is multiplied by the same 4: 4 × 4 mol Fe = 16 mol Fe and 4 × 3 mol O₂ = 12 mol O₂. The set 8 mol Fe + 6 mol O₂ only doubles the recipe, 4 mol Fe + 3 mol O₂ scales the product but leaves the reactants alone, and 12 mol Fe + 16 mol O₂ swaps the two reactant coefficients.
Question 10Intermediate

In the Sack of Silver reaction, 2AgNO₃(aq) + Cu(s) → Cu(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2Ag(s), the apprentice reacts 3.40 g of AgNO₃ with 0.64 g of Cu on a balance that reads to 0.01 g. Both reactants are used up and 2.16 g of Ag is collected. By the law of conservation of mass, what mass of Cu(NO₃)₂ formed?

  1. A. 4.04 g of Cu(NO₃)₂
  2. B. 1.88 g of Cu(NO₃)₂
  3. C. 2.16 g of Cu(NO₃)₂
  4. D. 1.9 g of Cu(NO₃)₂
Show answer
Answer: B. 1.88 g of Cu(NO₃)₂
Mass in must equal mass out: 3.40 g + 0.64 g = 4.04 g total, so the Cu(NO₃)₂ is 4.04 g − 2.16 g = 1.88 g. Adding and subtracting keeps the fewest decimal places, and every mass here is known to the hundredths place, so 1.9 g throws away a decimal place the balance actually supplies; 4.04 g is the combined reactant mass and 2.16 g is the silver that was already weighed separately.

  2. B. 4Fe + 6CuCl₂ → 4FeCl₃ + 6Cu

Question 12Advanced

A study guide prints the chalk reaction as “Na₂CO₃(aq) + CaCl₂(aq) → CaCO₃(aq) + 2NaCl(aq)” and reads it as “106.0 grams of sodium carbonate react with 111.0 grams of calcium chloride.” In the lab, the calcium carbonate is the white solid you scrape off the filter paper. Molar masses: Na₂CO₃ 106.0 g/mol, CaCl₂ 111.0 g/mol, CaCO₃ 100.1 g/mol. Which reading of this recipe is correct?

  1. A. One mole of Na₂CO₃ and one mole of CaCl₂ give one mole of CaCO₃, and every product stays dissolved in the beaker
  2. B. Two moles of Na₂CO₃ and two moles of CaCl₂ give one mole of CaCO₃, because the 2 in front of NaCl scales them
  3. C. One gram of Na₂CO₃ and one gram of CaCl₂ give one gram of CaCO₃, because the coefficients are counting out grams
  4. D. One mole of Na₂CO₃ and one mole of CaCl₂ give one mole of CaCO₃, and that CaCO₃ is the solid you filter
Show answer
Answer: D. One mole of Na₂CO₃ and one mole of CaCl₂ give one mole of CaCO₃, and that CaCO₃ is the solid you filter
Coefficients count moles, not grams, so the understood 1s mean that 1 mol of Na₂CO₃ and 1 mol of CaCl₂ give 1 mol of CaCO₃ and 2 mol of NaCl, and the chalk you scrape off the filter paper shows CaCO₃ is a solid, written (s). Reading the coefficients as grams ignores that 1 mol of Na₂CO₃ is 106.0 g while 1 mol of CaCO₃ is 100.1 g, and the 2 in front of NaCl belongs to NaCl alone, so it cannot turn the reactants into 2 mol each.

Mole Ratios from Coefficients

Question 13Beginner

The coefficients provide a ratio. In 3CuSO₄(aq) + 2Al(s) → Al₂(SO₄)₃(aq) + 3Cu(s), what ratio do the coefficients give for aluminum to copper?

  1. A. 2 mol Al : 1 mol Cu
  2. B. 3 mol Al : 2 mol Cu
  3. C. 1 mol Al : 1 mol Cu
  4. D. 2 mol Al : 3 mol Cu
Show answer
Answer: D. 2 mol Al : 3 mol Cu
Read the coefficient in front of each substance: Al carries a 2 and Cu carries a 3, so the ratio is 2 mol Al : 3 mol Cu. Writing 3 mol Al : 2 mol Cu flips the two numbers, 2 mol Al : 1 mol Cu borrows the 1 that belongs to Al₂(SO₄)₃, and 1 : 1 ignores the coefficients altogether.
Question 14Beginner

For the flash of magnesium in the furnace, 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO, what ratio do the coefficients give for magnesium to oxygen gas?

  1. A. 2 mol Mg : 2 mol O₂
  2. B. 1 mol Mg : 1 mol O₂
  3. C. 2 mol Mg : 1 mol O₂
  4. D. 1 mol Mg : 2 mol O₂
Show answer
Answer: C. 2 mol Mg : 1 mol O₂
Mg carries the coefficient 2 and O₂ has no written coefficient, which means 1, giving 2 mol Mg : 1 mol O₂. Writing 1 mol Mg : 2 mol O₂ turns the ratio upside down, 2 mol Mg : 2 mol O₂ copies the 2 from MgO onto the oxygen, and 1 : 1 ignores the coefficient in front of Mg.

The jar was mixed from 12.5 g of copper (II) sulfate pentahydrate, CuSO₄·5H₂O, molar mass 249.7 g/mol, with aluminum foil in excess. Molar mass Cu 63.55 g/mol. What mass of copper can form?

  1. A. 4.98 g Cu, from 12.5 ÷ 159.6 = 0.07832 mol, then × 63.55 g/mol
  2. B. 3.18 g Cu, from 12.5 ÷ 249.7 = 0.05006 mol, then × 63.55 g/mol
  3. C. 9.54 g Cu, from 0.05006 mol × 3 before multiplying by 63.55 g/mol
  4. D. 1.06 g Cu, from 0.05006 mol ÷ 3 before multiplying by 63.55 g/mol
Show answer
Answer: B. 3.18 g Cu, from 12.5 ÷ 249.7 = 0.05006 mol, then × 63.55 g/mol
Each CuSO₄·5H₂O unit carries five waters that add mass but no copper, so use 249.7 g/mol: 12.5 ÷ 249.7 = 0.05006 mol, and the 3-to-3 ratio carries that straight to copper, giving 0.05006 mol × 63.55 g/mol = 3.18 g to three digits.
