Signs of a Chemical Reaction

Question 1Beginner

A student pours two clear, colorless liquids together and steady bubbles rise from the mixture without any heating. Which sign of a chemical reaction is this?

  1. A. The formation of a gas
  2. B. A change in the volume
  3. C. A change in the texture
  4. D. A change in the shape
Show answer
Answer: A. The formation of a gas
Bubbles rising from a liquid that is not being heated show that a new gas is being produced, and formation of a gas is one of the signs of a chemical reaction. A change in shape or texture describes a physical change only, and a change in volume by itself does not show that new substances formed.
Question 2Beginner

Two clear solutions are mixed and a cloudy solid slowly settles to the bottom of the beaker. What is that solid called?

  1. A. A coefficient
  2. B. A precipitate
  3. C. A solute
  4. D. A reactant
Show answer
Answer: B. A precipitate
A solid that forms when two solutions are mixed is called a precipitate, and it is one of the signs of a chemical reaction. A solute dissolves instead of settling out, a reactant is a starting material, and a coefficient is a number written in front of a formula.
Question 3Beginner

Which of these observations is NOT a sign that a chemical reaction has taken place?

  1. A. The color changes from blue to green
  2. B. A solid is crushed into powder
  3. C. A new gas bubbles out of the liquid
  4. D. The mixture gives off heat and light
Show answer
Answer: B. A solid is crushed into powder
Crushing a solid changes only its size and shape, so no new substance is made and no chemical reaction has occurred. Releasing a new gas, giving off heat and light, and an unexpected color change are all listed as signs of a chemical reaction.
Question 4Beginner

A candle burns steadily, giving off both heat and light. Which sign of a chemical reaction does this show?

  1. A. The absorption of a gas
  2. B. A change in mass only
  3. C. The release of energy
  4. D. A change in state only
Show answer
Answer: C. The release of energy
Burning gives off energy as heat and light, and the release (or absorption) of heat or light is one of the signs of a chemical reaction. Melting wax is only a change of state, and the wax is not simply absorbing a gas or quietly losing mass.
Question 5Beginner

Two household liquids are poured into the same container and a sharp new smell appears right away. What is the best conclusion?

  1. A. A new substance formed, because any two liquids react when mixed
  2. B. No reaction occurred, because only solids can take part in reactions
  3. C. A new substance formed, because a change in odor signals a reaction
  4. D. No reaction occurred, because odors are always physical properties
Show answer
Answer: C. A new substance formed, because a change in odor signals a reaction
A brand-new odor means brand-new substances, and a change in odor is one of the listed signs of a chemical reaction. Odors are not always physical properties, liquids do not react every time they are mixed, and liquids can be reactants just as solids can.
Question 6Intermediate

A student heats a sealed sample and records four observations. Which observation is the best evidence that a chemical reaction happened rather than a physical change?

  1. A. Steam rises from the sample and condenses back into liquid water on the lid
  2. B. The solid sample cracks into smaller pieces as it is stirred with a rod
  3. C. A gas with a sharp new odor bubbles out and does not come back on cooling
  4. D. The solid dissolves completely and can be recovered by evaporating the water
Show answer
Answer: C. A gas with a sharp new odor bubbles out and does not come back on cooling
A chemical reaction makes new substances, so producing a gas with a new odor that will not reverse on cooling is the strongest evidence. Steam condensing back to liquid water is a change of state, cracking into smaller pieces changes only size, and a solid that can be recovered by evaporating the water was only dissolved, so none of those three make a new substance.

Reading a Chemical Equation

Question 8Beginner

In the equation 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l), which substances are the reactants?

  1. A. H₂ and O₂
  2. B. O₂ and H₂O
  3. C. H₂O only
  4. D. H₂ and H₂O
Show answer
Answer: A. H₂ and O₂
Reactants are written on the left side of the arrow, so hydrogen gas and oxygen gas are the reactants here. Water sits on the right side of the arrow, which makes it the product, so any choice that lists H₂O is naming a product instead.
Question 9Beginner

In the equation NaCl(aq) + AgNO₃(aq) → AgCl(s) + NaNO₃(aq), what does the symbol (aq) tell you about a substance?

  1. A. It is dissolved in water
  2. B. It is a gas escaping from the beaker
  3. C. It is a dry solid resting on the bottom
  4. D. It is a pure liquid with no water
Show answer
Answer: A. It is dissolved in water
The symbol (aq) stands for aqueous, meaning the substance is dissolved in water, while (l) marks a pure liquid, (g) a gas, and (s) a solid. In this equation AgCl(s) is the only solid, so reading (aq) as a dry solid would mix up the two products.
Question 10Beginner

What does the arrow in a chemical equation mean?

  1. A. Plus, so the substances on each side are added
  2. B. Yields, and it separates reactants from products
  3. C. Minus, so a substance is taken away from the mix
  4. D. Equals, so both sides hold the same substances
Show answer
Answer: B. Yields, and it separates reactants from products
The arrow is read as the word yields, and it separates the starting materials on the left from the substances made on the right. It does not mean the two sides hold the same substances; the same atoms are there, but they have been rearranged into new substances.
Question 11Beginner

In the equation CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g), which substance leaves the reaction as a gas?

  1. A. CaO
  2. B. CO₂
  3. C. CaCO₃
  4. D. O₂
Show answer
Answer: B. CO₂
The state symbol (g) is written after CO₂, so carbon dioxide is the substance that leaves as a gas. Both CaCO₃ and CaO carry the symbol (s) for solid, and O₂ never appears anywhere in this equation.
Question 12Beginner

In the formula H₂SO₄, what is the small 4 written just after the O called?

  1. A. A coefficient
  2. B. A product
  3. C. A subscript
  4. D. A state symbol
Show answer
Answer: C. A subscript
A small number written after an element's symbol is a subscript, and it tells how many atoms of that element the formula contains, so H₂SO₄ holds 4 oxygen atoms. A coefficient is a full-size number written in front of a formula, and a state symbol such as (aq) goes in parentheses after it.
Question 13Beginner

Which chemical equation matches the word equation "magnesium + oxygen yields magnesium oxide"?

  1. A. 2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s)
  2. B. 3Mg(s) + N₂(g) → Mg₃N₂(s)
  3. C. Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂(g)
  4. D. 2MgO(s) → 2Mg(s) + O₂(g)
Show answer
Answer: A. 2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s)
The words name magnesium and oxygen as the reactants and magnesium oxide as the product, which is exactly 2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s). The equation that starts with 2MgO(s) runs the change backwards, and the nitrogen and hydrochloric acid equations use reactants the words never mention.
Question 14Beginner

In the equation Zn(s) + 2HCl(aq) → ZnCl₂(aq) + H₂(g), which substance is a product?

  2. B. Zn(s)
  3. C. HCl(aq)
  4. D. Zn(s) and HCl(aq)

Answer: A. H₂(g)
Products are written after the arrow, so hydrogen gas is a product of this reaction. Zinc and hydrochloric acid sit to the left of the arrow, which makes them the reactants that get used up.
Question 15Intermediate

Which statement correctly describes the equation 2NaHCO₃(s) → Na₂CO₃(s) + H₂O(g) + CO₂(g)?

  1. A. Two solid reactants combine to form one solid product and two gaseous products
  2. B. One solid reactant breaks down into three solid products of equal amounts
  3. C. One solid reactant breaks down into one solid product and two gaseous products
  4. D. Two gaseous reactants combine to form one solid product and one liquid product
Show answer
Answer: C. One solid reactant breaks down into one solid product and two gaseous products
Everything before the arrow is the reactant, and only NaHCO₃(s) appears there, so there is one solid reactant; after the arrow are Na₂CO₃(s), H₂O(g), and CO₂(g), which is one solid and two gases. Reading the 2 in 2NaHCO₃ as two different reactants is the common slip, and calling all three products solids or the reactants gases ignores the (g) and (s) labels that are printed in the equation.
Question 16Intermediate

In the equation 2C₄H₁₀(g) + 13O₂(g) → 8CO₂(g) + 10H₂O(g), what does the 13 written in front of O₂ tell you?

  1. A. Thirteen atoms of oxygen in total are used up during the reaction
  2. B. Thirteen molecules, or 13 moles, of oxygen gas are used in the reaction
  3. C. Each molecule of oxygen gas in this reaction contains thirteen atoms
  4. D. The subscript on oxygen must be multiplied by thirteen when counting
Show answer
Answer: B. Thirteen molecules, or 13 moles, of oxygen gas are used in the reaction
A coefficient counts whole units of the substance that follows it, so 13O₂ means 13 molecules (or 13 moles) of O₂. Each O₂ molecule still holds only 2 atoms, which makes 13 × 2 = 26 oxygen atoms rather than 13 atoms, and a coefficient multiplies the entire formula instead of being folded into the subscript.
Question 17Intermediate

Use the equation 2Fe(NO₃)₃(aq) + 3Mn(s) → 2Fe(s) + 3Mn(NO₃)₂(aq). How many moles of manganese must react in order to produce 2 moles of iron?

  1. A. 1 mole of manganese
  2. B. 3 moles of manganese
  3. C. 2 moles of manganese
  4. D. 6 moles of manganese
Show answer
Answer: B. 3 moles of manganese
Coefficients are read as mole amounts, and the equation pairs 3Mn with 2Fe, so 3 moles of manganese produce 2 moles of iron. Answering 2 moles copies the coefficient on iron instead of reading the one on manganese, 6 moles multiplies the two coefficients together, and 1 mole assumes an unwritten coefficient that the equation does not have.
Question 18Intermediate

Which sentence reads the equation CuSO₄(aq) + Na₂CO₃(aq) → Na₂SO₄(aq) + CuCO₃(s) correctly?

  1. A. Solid copper (II) sulfate plus liquid sodium carbonate yields aqueous sodium sulfate plus solid copper (II) carbonate
  2. B. Aqueous sodium sulfate plus solid copper (II) carbonate yields aqueous copper (II) sulfate plus aqueous sodium carbonate
  3. C. Aqueous copper (II) sulfate plus aqueous sodium carbonate yields aqueous sodium sulfate plus solid copper (II) carbonate
  4. D. Aqueous copper (II) sulfate plus aqueous sodium carbonate yields solid sodium sulfate plus aqueous copper (II) carbonate

Answer: C. Aqueous copper (II) sulfate plus aqueous sodium carbonate yields aqueous sodium sulfate plus solid copper (II) carbonate
The reactants are both labeled (aq), and of the two products only CuCO₃ carries (s), so the solid that settles out is copper (II) carbonate. Assigning the (s) to sodium sulfate swaps the two labels, calling the reactants solid and liquid ignores the (aq) printed on both, and starting the sentence with sodium sulfate reads the equation backwards through the arrow.
Question 19Advanced

A study guide states: “In 4Fe(s) + 3O₂(g) → 2Fe₂O₃(s), the coefficients mean 4 grams of iron react with 3 grams of oxygen.” How should this equation actually be read?

  1. A. Four iron atoms react with three oxygen atoms to give two units of the oxide
  2. B. Four grams of iron react with three grams of oxygen gas, exactly as written
  3. C. Three iron atoms react with four oxygen molecules, reading the arrow backward
  4. D. Four iron atoms react with three oxygen molecules to give two oxide units
Show answer
Answer: D. Four iron atoms react with three oxygen molecules to give two oxide units
Coefficients count particles, not mass: 4 Fe atoms react with 3 O₂ molecules to give 2 formula units of iron (III) oxide, and the (s) and (g) labels give the states. Reading them as grams ignores that different atoms have different masses, and calling 3O₂ “three oxygen atoms” loses half the oxygen, since three molecules hold six atoms. The arrow always points from reactants to products, so iron and oxygen cannot be swapped to 3 Fe and 4 O₂.

Counting Atoms with Coefficients

Question 20Beginner

A balanced equation contains the term 3H₂O. How many hydrogen atoms does that term represent?

  1. A. 2 hydrogen atoms
  2. B. 3 hydrogen atoms
  3. C. 6 hydrogen atoms
  4. D. 5 hydrogen atoms
Show answer
Answer: C. 6 hydrogen atoms
Multiply the coefficient by the subscript: 3 × 2 = 6 hydrogen atoms. Answering 2 uses the subscript alone and ignores the coefficient, and 5 comes from adding 3 + 2 instead of multiplying.
Question 21Beginner

A balanced equation contains the term 5Al(OH)₃. How many oxygen atoms does that term represent?

  1. A. 5 oxygen atoms
  2. B. 15 oxygen atoms
  3. C. 3 oxygen atoms
  4. D. 45 oxygen atoms
Show answer
Answer: B. 15 oxygen atoms
Multiply the coefficient, the subscript outside the parentheses, and the understood 1 on oxygen: 5 × 3 × 1 = 15 oxygen atoms. Answering 3 counts only one formula unit, and 45 multiplies by the 3 a second time, as if oxygen carried its own subscript of 3.

Law of Conservation of Mass

Question 33Beginner

The law of conservation of mass says that atoms are only rearranged in a chemical reaction. What does that mean about the mass of the reactants and the mass of the products?

  1. A. The reactants always weigh more
  2. B. The products always weigh more
  3. C. The two masses are equal
  4. D. The two masses are unrelated
Show answer
Answer: C. The two masses are equal
Because no atoms are created and none are destroyed, the total mass of the reactants must equal the total mass of the products. Saying that one side always weighs more would require atoms to appear from nowhere or vanish during the reaction.
Question 34Beginner

In a sealed flask, 4.0 g of hydrogen reacts completely with 32.0 g of oxygen and water is the only product. What mass of water forms?

  1. A. 36.0 g
  2. B. 8.0 g
  3. C. 32.0 g
  4. D. 28.0 g
Show answer
Answer: A. 36.0 g
All of the mass of the reactants ends up in the product, so 4.0 g + 32.0 g = 36.0 g of water. The value 28.0 g comes from subtracting the masses instead of adding them, and 32.0 g ignores the hydrogen completely.
Question 35Beginner

A student burns a magnesium strip in an open dish and the white powder left behind has more mass than the strip did. Which statement explains this correctly?

  1. A. Mass was created as the metal gave off bright light
  2. B. The balance always reads high after something burns
  3. C. Heat from the flame added extra mass to the powder

Answer: D. Oxygen from the air combined with the magnesium
The powder contains the magnesium plus the oxygen it took from the air, so the mass gained equals the mass of oxygen that reacted and no mass was created. Heat and light are energy, not matter, so a flame cannot add mass to the product.
Question 36Beginner

A reaction in an open beaker gives off a gas, and the beaker and its contents lose mass. Does this break the law of conservation of mass?

  1. A. Yes, because mass is lost whenever a gas forms
  2. B. No, because gases have no mass of their own
  3. C. No, the escaping gas carries away that mass
  4. D. Yes, because the atoms in the gas were destroyed
Show answer
Answer: C. No, the escaping gas carries away that mass
The gas that bubbles out still has mass, and in a sealed container the total mass would not change at all, so the law still holds. The atoms in the gas were not destroyed, and gases do have mass just as solids and liquids do.
Question 37Intermediate

In the reaction Ca(s) + 2H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(aq) + H₂(g), 40 grams of calcium react completely with 36 grams of water and 2 grams of hydrogen gas are collected. What mass of calcium hydroxide forms?

  1. A. 74 grams
  2. B. 76 grams
  3. C. 38 grams
  4. D. 72 grams
Show answer
Answer: A. 74 grams
Mass is conserved, so the reactants total 40 g + 36 g = 76 g and the products must also total 76 g: 76 g − 2 g = 74 g of calcium hydroxide. Answering 76 g forgets to remove the hydrogen gas that left the mixture, 38 g subtracts the hydrogen from the calcium alone, and 72 g subtracts the 2 g twice.

Question 41Advanced

  2. B. 0.44 g, since the lead mass is subtracted from the mass of the iodine used
  3. C. 4.64 g, since every digit of both measured masses belongs in the answer
  4. D. 2.6 g, as the guide states, since a product cannot outweigh either reactant
Show answer
Answer: A. 4.6 g, since 2.1 + 2.54 = 4.64 and 2.1 g limits the sum to the tenths place
Mass is conserved, so all of the lead and all of the iodine end up in the product: 2.1 g + 2.54 g = 4.64 g. The 2.1 g mass is known only to the tenths place, so the sum is reported as 4.6 g; 4.64 g claims a hundredths digit the lead mass never had, 2.6 g throws away most of the mass, and 0.44 g comes from subtracting when the two reactants combine.
