Kinetic Molecular Theory

Question 1Beginner

Your Science Spiral lists the postulates of kinetic molecular theory for the molecules or atoms of an ideal gas. Which statement is one of those postulates?

  1. A. They are strongly attracted to other atoms and molecules
  2. B. They fill up most of the volume of the container they are in
  3. C. They are packed in fixed rows and move at one shared speed
  4. D. They are moving in random directions at different speeds
Show answer
Answer: D. They are moving in random directions at different speeds
Kinetic molecular theory says the particles of an ideal gas move in random directions at different speeds. Fixed rows at one shared speed describes an orderly solid rather than a gas, an ideal gas particle is not attracted to other atoms or molecules, and the postulates say gas particles have no volume relative to the size of the container.
Question 2Beginner

Kinetic molecular theory says the particles of an ideal gas undergo elastic collisions. What does an elastic collision mean here?

  1. A. The particles stretch out of shape and spring back afterward
  2. B. The particles lose a little energy at each and every bounce
  3. C. The particles lose no energy when they bounce off each other
  4. D. The particles stick together for a moment when they collide
Show answer
Answer: C. The particles lose no energy when they bounce off each other
An elastic collision is one in which the particles lose no energy when they bounce off each other, which is why an ideal gas never slows down on its own. Losing a little energy at each bounce would let the gas cool without any heat leaving it, sticking together would make the particles behave like a liquid, and gas particles are treated as small hard spheres that do not stretch out of shape.

Question 5Intermediate

  3. C. Work is done on the gas: molecules struck by the advancing boundary rebound faster, so the average kinetic energy rises.
  4. D. Friction between the gas molecules and the moving metal boundary generates heat, which the gas then soaks up from it.
Show answer
Answer: C. Work is done on the gas: molecules struck by the advancing boundary rebound faster, so the average kinetic energy rises.

Pressure and Its Units

  2. B. The gas particles being pulled downward by their own weight
  3. C. The gas particles dissolving slowly into the walls of the container
  4. D. The gas particles sticking to the walls and staying there

Answer: A. The gas particles colliding with the walls of the container
Pressure comes from gas particles colliding with the walls of the container, so more collisions or harder collisions mean more pressure. Boyle's Law (P and V)

Question 13Beginner

Match the description to its name: “The pressure of a gas is inversely related to its volume, if temperature and moles of gas remain constant.” Which named relationship is this?

  1. A. Avogadro's Law, V ∝ n
  2. B. Gay-Lussac's Law, P ∝ T
  3. C. Boyle's Law, P ∝ 1/V
  4. D. Charles' Law, V ∝ T
Show answer
Answer: C. Boyle's Law, P ∝ 1/V
Boyle's Law is the named proportionality P ∝ 1/V, the inverse relationship between pressure and volume at constant temperature and moles. Charles' Law V ∝ T ties volume to temperature, Gay-Lussac's Law P ∝ T ties pressure to temperature, and Avogadro's Law V ∝ n ties volume to moles of gas, so none of those three relate pressure to volume.

Question 17Beginner

  4. D. They happen less often in the smaller space, since each particle now has less room in which to move
Show answer
Answer: C. They happen more often in the smaller space, so the inside pressure rises to match the outside air
