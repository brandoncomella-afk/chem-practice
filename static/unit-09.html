Heat, Temperature, and Thermal Energy

Question 1Beginner

Your Science Spiral notes for the Stardust unit define one term as “the flow of thermal energy.” Which term is being defined?

  1. A. Thermal energy, the motion of the particles
  2. B. Specific heat, the energy per gram per degree
  3. C. Temperature, the flow of thermal energy
  4. D. Heat, the flow of thermal energy
Show answer
Answer: D. Heat, the flow of thermal energy
Heat is the flow of thermal energy from one place to another, which is exactly the wording of the definition. Temperature measures the average kinetic energy rather than any flow, thermal energy is the particle motion itself instead of its movement from place to place, and specific heat is the energy needed to raise 1 gram by 1 °C.

Endothermic and Exothermic

Question 12Beginner

The unit defines one kind of reaction as one that releases more energy when bonds form than it takes in to break bonds. Which reaction is that?

  1. A. Endothermic, and the water around it cools down
  2. B. Exothermic, and the water around it cools down
  3. C. Exothermic, and the water around it warms up
  4. D. Endothermic, and the water around it warms up
Show answer
Answer: C. Exothermic, and the water around it warms up
A reaction that gives off more energy forming bonds than it absorbs breaking them is exothermic, and that released energy warms the water nearby. Calling the same description endothermic reverses the definition, and an exothermic reaction cannot leave the water cooler because energy is flowing out of the reaction and into the water.
Question 13Beginner

Which description matches an endothermic reaction?

  1. A. It absorbs more energy breaking bonds than it releases forming them
  2. B. It releases more energy forming bonds than it absorbs breaking them
  3. C. It absorbs and releases exactly equal energy in every bond change
  4. D. It forms brand new bonds without ever breaking any of the old ones
Show answer
Answer: A. It absorbs more energy breaking bonds than it releases forming them
Endothermic means energy goes in overall, which happens when breaking bonds costs more than forming new ones pays back. Releasing more energy forming bonds than breaking them is the definition of exothermic, exactly equal energy would leave no temperature change at all, and every reaction must break old bonds before it can form new ones.

Question 18Beginner

The unit notes say chemical energy is stored in the bonds between atoms. Which statement about those bonds is correct?

  1. A. Forming bonds absorbs energy and breaking bonds releases energy
  2. B. Breaking bonds and forming bonds both absorb energy from the water
  3. C. Breaking bonds and forming bonds both release energy to the water
  4. D. Breaking bonds absorbs energy and forming bonds releases energy

Question 19Beginner

Which pair of physical changes are both endothermic?

  1. A. Freezing and evaporation
  2. B. Freezing and condensation
  3. C. Melting and evaporation
  4. D. Boiling and condensation
Show answer

Melting and evaporation both pull particles apart, and both take energy in, so both are endothermic. Specific Heat

Question 25Beginner

The unit defines specific heat as the amount of energy it takes to do what?

  1. A. Boil 1 gram of a substance sitting at its boiling point
  2. B. Raise the temperature of 1 gram of a substance by 1 °C
  3. C. Raise the temperature of any sample of a substance by 1 °C
  4. D. Melt 1 gram of a substance sitting at its melting point
Show answer
Answer: B. Raise the temperature of 1 gram of a substance by 1 °C
Specific heat is the energy required to raise 1 gram of a substance by 1 °C, which is why its units are J/g°C. Raising any sample by 1 °C would depend on how big the sample is, melting 1 gram at its melting point is the heat of fusion, and boiling 1 gram is the heat of vaporization.
Question 26Beginner

Which set of units belongs with a specific heat value such as the 4.18 for water?

  1. A. g°C/J, gram degrees per joule added
  2. B. J/g°C, joules per gram per degree
  3. C. J/g, joules per gram of substance
  4. D. kJ/g, kilojoules per gram melted
Show answer
Answer: B. J/g°C, joules per gram per degree
Specific heat is energy per gram per degree, so water is 4.18 J/g°C and never simply 4.18 J/g. Both J/g and kJ/g leave out the degree and are the units used for a heat of fusion or vaporization, and g°C/J turns the ratio upside down.
Question 27Beginner

A 25.0 g piece of copper wire absorbs 1.9 × 10² J of thermal energy and its temperature rises 20.0 °C. Using q = mcΔT, what is the specific heat of the copper?

  1. A. 7.6 J/g°C
  2. B. 9.5 J/g°C
  3. C. 2.6 J/g°C
  4. D. 0.38 J/g°C
Show answer
Answer: D. 0.38 J/g°C
Rearranged, c = q ÷ (m × ΔT) = 1.9 × 10² J ÷ (25.0 g × 20.0 °C) = 0.38 J/g°C, and the 1.9 × 10² J carries only two digits, so the answer does too. Dividing by the mass alone gives 7.6, dividing by the temperature change alone gives 9.5, and 2.6 comes from flipping the ratio and dividing 5.0 × 10² by 1.9 × 10².

First reported flame is not the same as first ignition, foam ignites readily at 400 °C from radiation overhead, and a void carrying wiring and combustible lining has plenty of fuel.

Answer: B. Locked and blocked exits cut the escape time available once flashover began to spread

Case file, lab check: a student investigator's worked solution reads: “The calorimeter held 0.125 kg of water and the temperature rose 14.0 °C, so q = 0.125 × 4.18 J/g°C × 14.0 = 7.32 J.” Water is 4.18 J/g°C, 1 kg = 1000 g and 1 kJ = 1000 J. What is q for the water?

  1. A. +7.32 J, since 0.125 kg is the mass measured and 4.18 J/g°C works with any mass unit
  2. B. +7.32 × 10⁶ J, since 0.125 kg has to be converted into 1.25 × 10⁵ g of water first
  3. C. +7.3 × 10³ J, since 125 g is the right mass but only two digits may be reported
  4. D. +7.32 × 10³ J, since q = m × c × ΔT needs mass in grams and 0.125 kg is 125 g

Answer: D. +7.32 × 10³ J, since q = m × c × ΔT needs mass in grams and 0.125 kg is 125 g
